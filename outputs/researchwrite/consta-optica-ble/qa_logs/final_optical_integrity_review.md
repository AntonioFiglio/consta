# Final optical and research-integrity review

Review date: 2026-11-06. Reviewer: independent subagent. Materials: `exports/proposal_final.md`, `experiments/optical-tags/README.md`, and the code/results reviewed in `qa_logs/optical_review.md`. No report or code edits were made in this review.

## Verdict: PASS

No critical technical or integrity correction is required before publishing this report as a **research proposal with a bounded synthetic experiment**. This is not a pass for clinical deployment or a physical camera prototype.

## Technical correspondence

- The report describes the implemented shared circular frame and central dot accurately, and explicitly says the dot is not an orientation anchor. Cyclic dictionary search matches the implementation.
- The finite family parameters, configured separation audit and conservative correction policy are identified as discrete experiment properties, not measured hospital capacity.
- The pipeline description matches source inspection: candidate contours and ellipses come from image pixels; `detect` receives only the image and public family. Renderer identities/transforms are used by the evaluation harness, not passed to the detector. No ground-truth leak was found.
- The report preserves failed projective and partial-cover fixtures and distinguishes downsample–upsample conditions, uniform dimming, affine approximation and general projective geometry.
- The table agrees with the inspected saved scenario records. The report states that another numeric verification checked the counts; that numerical-verification artifact was outside this review’s ownership and was not independently re-audited here.
- The held-out verification is correctly qualified as a separate synthetic sequence using the same renderer/family/decoder. It is not described as an independent physical test.
- The gallery is correctly described as an offline viewer of precomputed observations, not live browser decoding or camera acquisition.
- The text distinguishes assigned distinct ID, estimated camera reading and authenticity of the physical attachment. It makes no perfect-ID, unlimited-family or impossible-confusion claim.

## Source and origin integrity

The narrow claim that circular families can encode individual identities is supported by the previously verified CCTag and RUNE-Tag originals. The custom sector emulator is correctly distinguished from those algorithms and does not inherit their published performance.

The report discloses AI tools, directed primary-source search, subagent review, synthetic emulation and absence of a systematic review. It acknowledges historical/partial access and industrial involvement in the international-source dossier. It does not claim patent novelty, licensed clinical hardware, or commercial freedom of operation from authorial demo code.

No CCTag/RUNE/AprilTag/TopoTag library import or copied implementation was found in the reviewed emulator. General image-processing/numerical dependencies are identified. This is a code-review observation, not an exhaustive software-provenance or patent audit.

## BLE alternative fairness

The BLE recommendation is framed as an engineering hypothesis for mobile assets in approved areas, based primarily on reduced dependence on optical line of sight and real-world RTLS precedents. The report explicitly avoids claiming measured superiority in cost, precision, coverage or safety.

It distinguishes RF+IR historical cases from BLE cases, notes cloud dependencies, separates BLE proximity/RSSI from AoA requirements and preserves optically hidden-tag limitations. It also states refusal criteria if room coverage or coexistence fails. This is a fair comparison at the stated evidence level; it does not establish that an AoA implementation is selected or clinically ready.

## Ethics and patient/privacy boundary

The report states there were no patients, physical camera captures, hospital installations, exam tests, contracts or commercial contacts. The experiment uses synthetic images; no real patient-image dataset was reviewed or generated by the inspected code. There is therefore no undisclosed human-subject experiment in this artifact.

Privacy is named in the optical-comparison table and experiment README as an unresolved requirement. Because the next proposed capture is a non-assistential bench test, the absence of a full camera-governance protocol is **advisory**, not a publication blocker for this research report. It would become a blocking issue before any camera pilot that could capture identifiable staff, patients or clinical scenes.

## Advisory improvements

These do not change the PASS verdict:

1. Before physical optical work, explicitly state that camera processing can capture people even without face recognition. Define fields of view, local processing, masking, image retention/access and permission for any diagnostic frames. “Store events only” is a proposed control, not proof that no personal image data are processed.
2. For maximum method precision, “amostra regiões dos setores” could be written as “amostra pontos em diferentes raios e fases dos setores, agregados por mediana.” The current implementation does not integrate arbitrary sector areas. The existing wording is broad but does not invalidate the result.
3. Record Python/OpenCV/NumPy/Pillow versions and code/artifact hashes when freezing a reproducible release. The current seeded corpus is inspectable but not a fully pinned computational environment.
4. Keep the phrase “no identity substitutions observed” connected to the finite fixture set whenever the results are summarized orally or in slides. Do not silently omit the rejected projective/occlusion conditions in a pitch.
5. Keep BLE AoA as a candidate requiring its own hardware/configuration evaluation; the evidence does not make it an approved replacement for a basic RSSI test.

## Final boundary

The report may support selecting the next proof of concept. It does not authorize hospital installation, patient experimentation, RF operation in restricted areas, classification MR Safe/MR Conditional or purchase of hardware. Optical material safety and radio coexistence remain separate validation tasks.
